1. **Direction is correct, but the board is not yet release-ready.** Relocating an unconstrained anchoring via is the appropriate first choice, provided the move preserves:

- connectivity and plane access,
- annular ring and hole clearance,
- copper-to-edge and copper-to-component clearances,
- controlled-impedance requirements where applicable,
- thermal/current capacity for power vias, and
- the stated via construction.

However, the current state still has **eight clearance violations**. “Actively being closed” is not equivalent to closed, and the five via-in-pad violations are not automatically waived by selecting filled-and-capped processing. The final board should have zero unreviewed clearance violations, or each remaining violation must have a documented, fab-confirmed exception.

Also, after the three relocations, rerun DRC, refill zones, regenerate all fabrication outputs, and verify that the exported files correspond to the final board—not merely to the current pre-relocation board of record.

2. The separate-wing explanation is substantively valid, but I would not use “DNP” for these pads. They are populated interface pads whose electrical partner is outside this PCB; they are not do-not-populate components.

The release package should explicitly document them as **external-board interface connections**, including:

- the exact 22 pads/nets,
- the mating wing-board design references,
- the fact that no same-board copper connection is expected,
- the connector or assembly boundary, and
- the expected connection when the wing board is installed.

For KiCad acceptance, use the appropriate intentional-unconnected treatment where applicable—such as schematic no-connect markers or explicit DRC exclusions/waivers—but do not change a real external interface net into a false NC net merely to suppress DRC. The two same-net zone/via items should also be documented as intentional and verified after zone refill. The F33_SCK residual remains a real tracked item and should either be corrected or receive a specific engineering waiver.

3. For the three dense-locality cases, **relocating the anchoring via is preferred** over forcing a trace through the existing via cluster. Use the same method as the DIO1 correction, but evaluate the complete local clearance graph before committing the move. A good relocation should clear all neighboring objects with margin rather than merely moving the violation to another pair.

If relocation cannot provide margin, the next preferable topology changes are, in order:

1. reroute the escape and change the layer transition location;
2. move the via cluster as a group while preserving the intended return-current path;
3. use a different supported via size only if the fabricator’s capability and reliability requirements permit it; and
4. revise the component escape topology if the locality is structurally over-constrained.

Do not resolve these by retaining sub-rule clearances without a written manufacturing and engineering justification.

4. **IPC-4761 Type VII filled-and-capped via-in-pad processing addresses solder wicking and planarity; it does not, by itself, waive copper-clearance rules.** Therefore, the five 0.125/0.150 mm clearances against a 0.20 mm rule are not automatically acceptable merely because the vias are filled and capped.

They can be released only if all of the following are true:

- the exact JLCPCB service and stackup support the geometry;
- JLCPCB confirms in writing that those local copper relationships are manufacturable under the selected filled-and-capped process;
- the locations are identified in the fabrication notes or a controlled exception table;
- the DRC violation is explicitly documented as an approved local-rule exception; and
- the exception does not create solder-mask, breakout, drill-to-copper, or assembly-reliability problems.

The fab-readiness document should therefore include a table listing each affected via/pad pair, measured clearance, applicable default rule, reason for the exception, and the corresponding JLCPCB confirmation/order evidence. If JLCPCB will not explicitly accept the geometry, the vias or neighboring pads must be relocated or the clearance must be made compliant.

Accordingly, the overall review direction is sound, and the manufacturing documentation is substantially improved. It is not yet an approval because the three dense-locality clearances remain open, the via-in-pad clearances require explicit fab-backed exceptions rather than only a process selection, and the final outputs must be regenerated after the last board edits.
