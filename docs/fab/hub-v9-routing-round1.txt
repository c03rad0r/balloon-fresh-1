## A. What is causing the congestion?

### U1 and U4 are close, but that is not inherently wrong
Your measured **1.99 mm gap means no overlap**. With so many connections between them, keeping these devices near each other is sensible. The 14.5 mm MOSI endpoint separation is not, by itself, an unreasonable PCB connection length.

However, a footprint-to-footprint gap is **not a measurement of available routing capacity**. Pad escapes, via clearances, nearby components and already occupied tracks determine that capacity.

Spatially, U4 sits in a constrained pocket:

- **North:** U1’s bottom pad row.
- **West:** the large U2 footprint and, farther south, U5.
- **East:** D_BP4 and the right-edge component column.
- **South:** D_HUB1, L4 and other parts.

That arrangement leaves limited freedom to build a good radio support cluster, even though the overall board is not full.

### The stronger placement objection is the scattered radio support network
In Image 1:

- **L1 is on the far-left edge**, while U4 is near the right side.
- **C0 is in the right-edge column, substantially below U4.**
- **L4 sits below D_HUB1**, rather than immediately alongside U4’s regulator pins.

Using the 70 mm outline as a visual scale, the labeled L1 is roughly **50 mm** from the radio RF pad, and C0 roughly **16 mm** away. These are screenshot estimates, not board-file measurements.

**If those labeled footprints are indeed the matching components you identify, this is a definite placement problem independent of the routing failures.** A matching network spread across that distance is not a close-to-chip match. Likewise, routing U4’s internal regulator connection around intervening components is a poor starting geometry for a compact regulator loop.

### What your experiments establish—and what they do not
The failure swapping is evidence of **competition for routing resources under the current placement and routing rules**:

- More rip-up does not fix it.
- Prioritizing two nets merely displaces failures onto other nets.
- The displaced nets include RF and the internal regulator connection, which should not be treated as freely detourable general-purpose connections.

It does **not** prove that the board is mathematically unroutable, or that the outline must grow. Nor does it prove that the 1.99 mm gap is the particular bottleneck.
